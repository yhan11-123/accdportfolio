Kindle carries fifteen million books and one layout. Open any of them and you get a column of text, with whatever images the book has scaled down to sit between the paragraphs. That works for a novel. It doesn't work for a book you bought because of how it looks. I spent time with Kindle's illustrated catalogue and with the people who buy from it. Both pointed in the same direction: the format treats images as supporting material, while readers treat them as the reason the book exists. Kindle TV moves those books to the screen that can actually hold an image, and leaves the phone the job a remote handles worst: aiming a loupe at one spot inside the picture.

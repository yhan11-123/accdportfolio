Younhee Han

Kindle carries fifteen million books and one layout: a column of text, with images scaled down between the paragraphs. That works for a novel, not for a book you bought because of how it looks. Kindle TV moves those books to the screen that can actually hold an image, and leaves the phone the job a remote handles worst: aiming a loupe at one spot inside the picture.

02 · Research

Four kinds of reader.

  • Casual readers who read when they have time or feel motivated
  • Digital readers who use phones, tablets, or reading apps
  • Students who balance reading with school, work, and other responsibilities
  • Social readers who discover or discuss books through friends, reviews, or online spaces

03 · The problems

Three things Kindle still makes hard.

The third has never been treated as a design problem.

Finding the next book

Browsing is a grid of small covers and titles you already have to know. Interest fades before a choice is made.

Keeping what you found

Highlights collect in a flat list with no shape. After the last page, nothing carries the book forward.

Holding every kind of book

Kindle carries novels, cookbooks and design monographs alike. One text-first frame has to serve them all.

The key insight

“Kindle can hold a book's words but not its images, and every friction a reader feels traces back to that gap.”

01 · On screen

Discovery home

Covers carry the first impression, so the home screen is nothing but covers. Browse by cover, then sample before you commit.

The thought

Not a second screen

The phone never shows the book. It carries the parts that need a fingertip, like pointing, framing, and saving, and leaves the looking to the wall.

02 · On screen

Connect TV

One code pairs the two devices. The television opens the book the moment the code is accepted.

The thought

Built for ten feet away

One constraint: a remote and three metres of air.

03 · On screen

Auto Reading

Text advances on its own. The remote only pauses it, or changes the pace — reading becomes something you watch, not something you operate.

The thought

The composition survives

A handheld cuts a two-page composition in half. A wide screen gives it back.

04 · On screen

05 · On screen

The wall gives the size. The hand gives the aim.

The phone moves a loupe across the spread, so detail and context stay on the wall together. One reading, two instruments.

06 · On screen

Note taking, then Wrap-up

A note is pinned to the paragraph in focus and typed on the phone, while the page stays on the wall. At the last page the session is counted: time, highlights, notes.

The thought

Look at it together

One plate, several people, no one holding anything.

07 · On screen

Book Club

Members read apart, then meet in front of one screen. Each session opens with “Previously on” and closes into the archive.

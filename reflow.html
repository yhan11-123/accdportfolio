Reflow

A day planner for the moment the day stops going to plan.

PROBLEM
One task runs long, and the rest of the plan quietly stops being true.
SOLUTION
Reflow catches the overrun and rebuilds the rest of the day as one proposal you accept in a tap.
ROLE
Solo · research, UX, UI, development, launch
PLATFORM
iOS, with a Live Activity on the lock screen

01THE PROBLEM

Thirty minutes becomes an hour, and nothing in the day absorbs the difference.

Each miss feeds the next. The plan never gets a chance to catch up.

01WHAT THE DAY FEELS LIKE

By the third broken day, people stop adjusting the plan and start abandoning it.

  1. Thinks

    “If I move this, what else has to move?”

  2. Says

    “I’ll just do it tomorrow.”

  3. Does

    Stops opening the planner once the day is off.

  4. Feels

    Resigned, and starts to plan less.

02THE INSIGHT

People don’t quit planning because the plan is wrong. They quit because fixing it costs too much.

HOW MIGHT WE

How might we make re-planning after an overrun cost one tap, so a broken hour doesn’t become a lost day?

CaptureJust enough about each task to plan with
NoticeThe overrun, as it happens
RebuildThe rest of the day, for the user to accept

03THE FIX

Same overrun, same afternoon. One task moves instead of four.

04THE FLOW

From a plan, to an overrun, to a rebuilt day, in six screens.

  1. 01TodayTimed and untimed tasks in one list. The day is a sequence, not a grid.
  2. 02Make a taskA name is enough. Everything else is optional.
  3. 03During a taskElapsed time, always next to the estimate.
  4. 04Task pendingThe task ran over. It stays in place, with three ways out.
  5. 05RescheduleThe rest of the day, rebuilt as one proposal.
  6. 06End of dayDone, ran over, moved. Counted, never scored.

05DESIGN DECISIONS

Every screen follows one rule: the day can change, but the user decides.

DECISION 1

Ask for a name, not a form

Adding a task takes a name. Duration and importance sit one step further in, because they’re what the reschedule reads later, not what you need to get started.

NOT
A full form on every task, so quick capture gets skipped.
INSTEAD
Progressive detail: optional fields that pay off when the day changes.

DECISION 2

Going over is a number, not an alarm

Elapsed time never appears alone. It sits against the estimate you set, so running over is something you’re already watching, on the screen and on the lock screen.

NOT
A warning that interrupts, the moment you’re already behind.
INSTEAD
A live timer with the estimate beside it, plus a Live Activity.

DECISION 3

One proposal, not a menu

When the day breaks, comparing options is one more job. Reflow hands back a single rebuilt day and shows where every task lands before anything moves.

NOT
Several plans to compare, or tasks moved silently in the background.
INSTEAD
One proposal, every move visible, one tap to accept.
  1. ReadsUnfinished tasks, duration, importance, fixed times
  2. GeneratesEvery schedule that fits the time remaining
  3. SelectsOne proposal, ranked by importance and fit
  4. ReturnsWhere each task lands, before you accept

DECISION 4

Rules move the day. AI only estimates.

The reschedule is deterministic, so the same day always gives the same answer and you can trust it. AI comes in at the one place judgment beats rules: guessing how long a task you’ve never done will take.

NOT
An AI that rearranges your day for reasons you can’t check.
INSTEAD
Predictable rules for moving tasks, AI for a first estimate you can edit.

WHAT SHIPPED AI costs money on every call. To keep Reflow free, the App Store version estimates from your own history instead: the median of how long similar tasks took.

DECISION 5

Overdue, not failed

What didn’t finish carries to tomorrow as a task, with its category and duration intact. There’s no pile of missed things, and no day ends in a score. This is the answer to “resigned, and starts to plan less.”

NOT
A backlog of red, overdue items that grows every day.
INSTEAD
Carried and postponed states, and an end of day that counts without judging.

06BEHIND THE SCREEN

Designing the app meant designing its data. Every mark on a row is a column.

Reflow runs on Supabase (Postgres): ten tables, each locked to its owner, and 47 migrations, because the schema changed every time the design did.

The schema followed the design

  1. “If the day slips” · 3 options→Do it by · Today or Another dayThe middle option was ambiguous. Two choices, stored as a date.
  2. End-of-day feeling · 4 values→3 valuesToo packed, just right, time to spare.
  3. Categories→Areas + importance 0–5Where a task lives, and how much it matters, as two separate answers.
